Basecamp and HEY

Built on the apps you already live in.

Daybreak isn't one more place to keep work. It reads what's already on your plate, lets you decide when each thing happens, and sends the result back where it belongs.

Basecamp

Comes in

  • To-dos assigned to you
  • Schedule entries
  • Card table cards, with their stage
  • Descriptions and comments, right on the card

Goes back

  • Check off a to-do and it's done in Basecamp
  • Finish a card and it moves to Done

HEY Optional

Comes in

  • Your HEY Calendar, beside your to-dos
  • Imbox, Reply Later and Set Aside
  • Any email, as a to-do

Goes back

  • Timeboxes become calendar events, reminders and all
  • Your evening note goes to your HEY Journal

The week

The whole week, on one screen.

Every day fits at any width, so nothing hides off to the right. Drag a to-do from Tuesday to Thursday, or into Sometime this week when it doesn't need a day yet.

  • Finish with a stamp. Pick the one you like. Done should feel done.
  • Goals and a daily log. Three goals for the week, and a running note for each day.
  • In your pocket, too. One day at a time, with Week, Today, Inbox and Log under your thumb.

Rituals

Open the day. Close the day.

Two short rituals bookend your day. A few minutes each, and they're why the rest of it feels lighter.

Sunrise · Morning

Good morning, Kosta.

Look back at yesterday, bring along what didn't get done, and choose what today is for.

  1. Look back
  2. Choose today
  3. Timebox it

Sundown · Evening

How did today go?

Check off what you finished, move what you didn't, and write a line about the day.

Saved to your HEY Journal

Roadmap's drafted. The design review ran long, but it was worth it.

  • Optional soundtrack. A soft score for each ritual, made live in your browser.
  • Setup is a sunrise too. You connect Basecamp and HEY while the sky lightens.

Focus

One to-do. Nothing else on screen.

Open any to-do in Focus and the week steps aside. A timer, your private notes, and the Basecamp description and comments for when you need them.

How it's built

Small, quiet, and yours.

  • Just for you

    One person, one plan. Your team keeps working in Basecamp, and Daybreak never asks them for anything.

  • Quiet

    No notifications, no badges, no streaks. It's there when you open it.

  • Private

    Your plan lives in one SQLite file on your server. No analytics in the app, or on this page.

  • Open source

    MIT licensed. Read every line, change what you like, and run it for as long as you want.

Self-host

Run it on your own server.

Daybreak is one Rails app and one SQLite file. A laptop, a $5 server or Railway all work fine.

With Docker

  1. Create a Basecamp integration

    At launchpad.37signals.com/integrations, with the redirect URL http://localhost:3000/auth/basecamp/callback.

  2. Clone, add its keys to .env, and start 
    git clone https://github.com/is2b007/daybreak.git
cd daybreak && cp .env.example .env
docker compose up -d
  3. Open localhost:3000

    Onboarding connects your accounts. HEY is optional.

Or in one click

Deploy to Railway for about $5 a month. Set RAILS_MASTER_KEY, BASECAMP_CLIENT_ID and BASECAMP_CLIENT_SECRET, and add a volume at /rails/storage so your data survives deploys.
